I build software, and I spend a fair amount of time trying to break other
people's. This is where I write both of those down properly.

The bar is simple: everything here is something I actually did, with the
commands and numbers that came out of it. If I ran a lab, the output is in the
post. If a result looked too good, I say what I suspect instead of celebrating.
If I got something wrong, the post says so.

## What goes here

- **Guides** — things I worked out the slow way, written so the next person
  doesn't have to. Labs, tooling, workflows, and the commands that finally
  worked.
- **Notes** — shorter: a build that broke interestingly, a design decision I'd
  defend, something in my setup that turned out to matter more than expected.
- **Opinion** — the bug bounty ecosystem, tooling economics, where the practice
  is heading. Argued, not asserted.
- **Writeups** — findings, once they're allowed to be public.

## What doesn't, yet

Some of the most interesting work I do is on live bug bounty programs, and none
of that can be written up while it's live. Program rules generally say the same
thing: nothing public until the finding is resolved and publication is
permitted. So the material sits, and the writeup follows the disclosure, not the
other way around.

That's why the [disclosure policy](/disclosure/) exists, and why the site's
build enforces it rather than trusting me to remember. Posts about method —
how I scope, how I test, how I decide to stop — go up whenever they're ready,
because they describe what I do rather than what I found. Posts about findings
wait for permission.

The gap is real, and I'd rather explain it than quietly post something I
shouldn't have.

## Who I am

Cael. I write application code and I do security research on the side. The
[about page](/about/) has the longer version.

If something here is wrong, tell me and I'll correct it and say that I did. If
you want to report something in something I run, email rather than posting it
publicly, and I'll be honest about whether I can fix it.
